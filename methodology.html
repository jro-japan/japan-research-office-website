METHODOLOGY

Primary-source research with explicit evidence boundaries.

The method is designed to make the path from source to conclusion reviewable—and to make uncertainty visible rather than silently filling gaps.

RESEARCH PROCESS

From scope to delivery

  1. 1

    Scope the business question

    Define the entities, geography, date range, decision context, required evidence and deliverable.

  2. 2

    Collect primary and authoritative sources

    Prioritize official releases, corporate disclosures, government and regulatory records, standards or certification sources and other authoritative material appropriate to the question.

  3. 3

    Verify and cross-check

    Check entity identity, dates, figures, units, source context, document status and conflicts between sources.

  4. 4

    Normalize the evidence

    Structure evidence so claims can be compared and traced back to the underlying source.

  5. 5

    Separate evidence states

    Confirmed Facts are directly supported. Supported Inferences state the reasoning and basis. Unknowns identify what the available evidence does not establish.

  6. 6

    Deliver within the evidence boundary

    Present the evidence, interpretation and remaining questions without converting an incomplete signal into an unsupported conclusion.

NON-EQUIVALENCE

A signal is not automatically the business event someone hopes it proves.

Project exists ≠ Procurement started

Tender exists ≠ Supplier selected

Award exists ≠ Equipment OEM identified

Violation exists ≠ Equipment purchase required

M&A exists ≠ Supplier replacement

Product exit ≠ Replacement purchase

Financing approved ≠ Equipment purchased

Where the evidence stops, JRO says so.

JRO does not substitute its research for legal, engineering, compliance, procurement, financial or other professional judgment.
